5-Minute Scenario Starter: "Who Should Participate in a Study?"

Imagine a research team wants to study how social media algorithms affect teenager self-esteem in Guatemala City. If they only survey 20 students from a single elite private school, can they claim their results apply to all teenagers across the country? Why or why not?

Target Population

The entire group of individuals about whom the researcher wants to draw conclusions (e.g., all teenagers in Guatemala).

Study Sample

A smaller subgroup selected from the population to actually participate in the research study.

Representativeness

The extent to which the sample accurately mirrors the key demographic characteristics of the broader target population.

Sampling Strategies

Random Sampling: Every population member has an equal probability of selection.

Stratified Sampling: Subdividing population into subgroups (strata) and sampling proportionally.

Convenience Sampling: Selecting readily accessible participants (high risk of bias).

Generalizability Power
Random sampling minimizes selection bias

Informed Consent & Freedom

Informed Consent: Explicit permission granted with full knowledge of risks, purpose, and rights.

Assent (Minors): Agreement from minors in addition to parental consent.

Voluntary Participation: Right to withdraw at any moment without penalty or coercion.

Autonomy Protection
Prevents coercion & protects human rights

Privacy & Protection from Harm

Anonymity vs. Confidentiality: Anonymity keeps identities unknown; confidentiality keeps known data secure.

Minimizing Harm: Protecting participants from physical, psychological, or social distress.

Debriefing: Disclosing full study context and restoring baseline post-experiment.

Participant Welfare Index
Ensures zero psychological distress

Research Ethics Safeguards & Principles Overview

Ethical Principle Core Definition Violated Scenario Example Required Researcher Action
Informed Consent Participants know aims, procedures, risks, and rights before consenting. Testing a new study technique without informing students they are in an experiment. Provide written consent forms outlining rights and risks prior to testing.
Voluntary Withdrawal Freedom to leave the study at any time without loss of credit or penalty. Telling participants they will fail a course grade if they quit the survey midway. Explicitly state in writing and verbally that leaving causes zero negative consequences.
Anonymity & Privacy Ensuring personal identities cannot be linked to published research data. Publishing survey scores with students' full names and classroom seat numbers attached. Assign unique code numbers (e.g., Participant #104) and store data in password-protected drives.
Minimizing Harm & Debriefing Safeguarding emotional wellbeing and explaining full details after testing. Inducing severe stress during a test without explaining the study's true purpose afterward. Conduct post-experiment debriefing, answer questions, and offer mental health support.
